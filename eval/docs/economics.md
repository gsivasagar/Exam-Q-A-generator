# Introductory Economics

## Markets
The law of demand says that, other things equal, a higher price reduces the quantity demanded. The law of supply says that a higher price increases the quantity that producers offer. Market equilibrium occurs at the price where quantity supplied equals quantity demanded. A price ceiling set below equilibrium creates a shortage, and a price floor above equilibrium creates a surplus. Price elasticity of demand measures how strongly quantity demanded responds to a change in price, and goods with close substitutes tend to be elastic.

## Macroeconomics
Gross domestic product is the total market value of all final goods and services produced in a country in a year. Real GDP is adjusted for inflation, whereas nominal GDP uses current prices. Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level and is commonly measured by the consumer price index. Unemployment is split into frictional, structural and cyclical types, and cyclical unemployment rises in recessions. A recession is often defined as two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP.

## Policy
Fiscal policy uses government spending and taxation to influence the economy, and expansionary fiscal policy increases spending or cuts taxes. Monetary policy is run by the central bank, which can raise interest rates to cool inflation or lower them to encourage borrowing. Open market operations are the buying and selling of government bonds to change the money supply. The multiplier effect means that an initial increase in spending leads to a larger increase in national income. Quantitative easing creates new money to buy financial assets when interest rates are already near zero.

## Trade and Choice
Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative given up when a choice is made. Comparative advantage explains why countries gain from trade by specialising in goods they produce at a lower opportunity cost, even if another country is better at everything. A tariff is a tax on imports that protects domestic producers but raises prices for consumers. The law of diminishing marginal utility says that each additional unit consumed gives less extra satisfaction than the previous one. A monopoly is a single seller with market power that can restrict output and charge a higher price.
