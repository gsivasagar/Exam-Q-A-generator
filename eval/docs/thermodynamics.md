# Thermodynamics

## Laws
The zeroth law says that two systems each in thermal equilibrium with a third are in equilibrium with each other, which makes temperature measurable. The first law of thermodynamics is conservation of energy and states that the change in internal energy equals the heat added minus the work done by the system. The second law says that the total entropy of an isolated system never decreases, so heat does not flow spontaneously from a cold body to a hot body. The third law states that entropy approaches a constant minimum as temperature approaches absolute zero. Absolute zero is 0 kelvin, equal to minus 273.15 degrees Celsius.

## Gases
The ideal gas law is PV = nRT, linking pressure, volume, amount of gas and temperature. Boyle's law states that at constant temperature the pressure of a gas is inversely proportional to its volume. Charles's law states that at constant pressure the volume of a gas is proportional to its absolute temperature. The universal gas constant R is about 8.314 joules per mole per kelvin. In an isothermal process the temperature stays constant, whereas in an adiabatic process no heat is exchanged with the surroundings.

## Heat Engines
A heat engine converts heat into work by taking heat from a hot reservoir and rejecting some to a cold reservoir. The Carnot efficiency is one minus the cold temperature divided by the hot temperature, with both measured in kelvin, and it is the maximum possible for any engine between those reservoirs. A refrigerator moves heat from a cold space to a warm one by consuming work. The coefficient of performance measures how much heat a refrigerator or heat pump moves per unit of work. The Otto cycle models the four-stroke petrol engine, and the Diesel cycle models compression ignition.

## Heat Transfer
Conduction transfers heat through direct contact of particles, and metals conduct well because of their free electrons. Convection transfers heat by the bulk movement of a fluid, as when warm air rises. Radiation transfers energy as electromagnetic waves and needs no medium, which is how the Sun warms the Earth. The specific heat capacity of water is about 4200 joules per kilogram per kelvin, which is unusually high. Latent heat is the energy absorbed or released during a phase change without a change in temperature.
