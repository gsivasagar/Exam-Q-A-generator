# The French Revolution

## Causes
France faced a severe financial crisis in the 1780s caused by war debts and an unfair tax system in which the nobility and clergy paid little. Poor harvests raised the price of bread and increased hunger in the cities. Enlightenment thinkers such as Rousseau and Montesquieu challenged absolute monarchy and argued for popular sovereignty. The society was divided into three estates, and the Third Estate, made up of commoners, held the least power despite being the largest group. King Louis XVI summoned the Estates-General in May 1789 to approve new taxes.

## Early Revolution
The Third Estate declared itself the National Assembly in June 1789 and swore the Tennis Court Oath not to disband until France had a constitution. The Bastille prison was stormed by a Parisian crowd on 14 July 1789, a date still celebrated as the national holiday. In August 1789 the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen proclaimed liberty, equality before the law and national sovereignty. The Women's March on Versailles in October 1789 forced the royal family to move to Paris. The Civil Constitution of the Clergy of 1790 placed the Catholic Church under state control.

## Radical Phase
The monarchy was abolished in September 1792 and France became a republic. Louis XVI was executed by guillotine in January 1793 after being convicted of treason. The Committee of Public Safety led by Maximilien Robespierre ran the Reign of Terror, during which tens of thousands were executed. The Terror ended after Robespierre himself was arrested and guillotined in July 1794 during the Thermidorian Reaction. The Levee en masse of 1793 conscripted citizens to defend the republic against foreign armies.

## Napoleon
The Directory, a five-man executive, governed France from 1795 but was weak and corrupt. Napoleon Bonaparte seized power in the coup of 18 Brumaire in November 1799 and became First Consul. The Napoleonic Code of 1804 standardised French civil law and guaranteed equality before the law. Napoleon crowned himself Emperor in December 1804. His defeat at Waterloo in June 1815 ended his rule and led to his exile on Saint Helena.
